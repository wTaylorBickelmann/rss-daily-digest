cs.AI’s recent list mixed safety evaluations with systems papers that try to spend fewer tokens for the same downstream score. The through-line is measurement: several abstracts spend as much space defining what they *won’t* claim as on the method.

If you only skim one theme, make it process supervision versus outcome supervision. Two preprints circle the same idea from opposite directions — one arguing that step-level labels overfit to annotator style, the other that outcome-only RL hides mode collapse until deployment.

## Papers

- [Process vs. outcome supervision under noisy labels](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01234) — A controlled comparison on math and code with deliberately corrupted traces.
- [Cache-aware decoding for long RSS contexts](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04567) — Practical KV-cache batching when every item in a feed must be cited.
- [A checklist for not overclaiming LLM evals](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.07890) — Short, opinionated, and aimed at reviewers as much as authors.
